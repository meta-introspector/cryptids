A plain-language guide

A big math idea,
one tiny step at a time.

This project pairs number calculations with a tiny computer called a Turing machine. The machine reads a tape, changes one square, and moves left or right. This page lets you see what those words mean.

Number explorer

Enter a number n. The page calculates a product built from factorial and primes, then shows its digit count and a few related values.

Aₙ = n! × P(n)

P(n) means multiply all prime numbers up to n. n! means 1 × 2 × … × n.

What does the product mean?

n! multiplies every whole number up to n. P(n) multiplies every prime up to n. The calculator multiplies those two results.

A helpful distinction

What the browser can do

The JavaScript page can calculate the number definitions and run a small machine from rules. The Lean source also contains formal proofs; those proofs are mathematical arguments, not steps the browser can run.

Included: exact numeric calculations and an editable example tape machine.

Not included: Lean proof checking and the full 121-state machine transition table.

At a glance

Source ideas translated

  • Primorial: product of prime numbers no larger than n.
  • Shunia product: n factorial multiplied by the primorial.
  • Quartering clock: repeatedly divide n − 1 by four and count the divisions.
  • Machine step: read a symbol, apply a rule, write, move, and change state.
